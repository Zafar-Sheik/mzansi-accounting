# Learning design & study tools

Accounting is a *doing* subject. Every lesson is built so learners spend most of their time predicting, sorting, posting and calculating – not copying notes.

## The six-step lesson

1. **Hook.** A "Mzansi moment" or a Money Life story: Thandi's spaza, a car wash, a first payslip, a store card, tax season.
2. **Do it hands-on.** A lab: tip the accounting-equation scale, post a T-account, drag accounts into a Balance Sheet, reconcile a bank statement, run a side hustle. Every lab has an *Explain* box in plain language.
3. **Practise it.** Worked examples revealed **one step at a time**, each with a "Why?". Then typed-answer practice with hints and a full solution, and an endless drill.
4. **Check it.** A quick quiz with an explanation for every answer, plus flashcards of the key terms.
5. **Exam it.** NSC-style questions with memos in the Exam Room and mock papers.
6. **Celebrate.** XP, streaks, badges and confetti for finished labs.

## Vocational and hands-on learning

- **Real documents.** The source-document sorter shows receipts, duplicate invoices, credit notes and vouchers the way they look in a real business.
- **Real tools.** The payslip builder and tax calculator use the real SARS tables (2025/26) – learners can use them for their own first job.
- **Run a business.** The side-hustle simulator makes learners buy stock, set prices, react to paydays and rain, keep a cash book and read their own Income Statement.
- **Accounting for life.** The Money Life track links every CAPS skill to adult life: bank reconciliation → checking your own statement; depreciation → your first car; ratios → investing.

## Why these tools?

| Tool | Research idea |
|---|---|
| Predict-then-post labs | Generation effect – predicting first makes the answer stick |
| Worked examples, step by step | Worked-example effect; lowers cognitive load |
| Typed answers with specific feedback | Retrieval practice with immediate corrective feedback |
| Sorters, matches and sequences | Active categorisation; good for kinaesthetic learners |
| Spaced flashcards (Leitner boxes) | Spacing effect |
| Mixed Quiz Arena and mock papers | Interleaving; exam-condition practice |
| Weak-spot coach | Metacognition – knowing what you don't know |

## Exam technique built in

The Exam Room explains the action verbs (name, calculate, explain, comment, identify a problem, suggest), shows memos tick by tick and repeats the golden rule for comments: **quote the figures, explain, then advise**.
